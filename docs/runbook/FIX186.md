# FIX186: the gate stops its check groups through the bounded-run primitive (slice 1)

## What happened

FIX122 made the bench core's bounded-run primitive (`scripts/bench/core/common.sh`) the only
TERM, grace, KILL code for the harness callers, and `tests/check-bounded-run.sh` refuses a
literal KILL and a `kill -0` poll outside it. The gate's own stop was outside both:

- `gate_end` in `scripts/gate-checks.sh` ran `gate_kill TERM`, polled `gate_alive` with
  `sleep 0.2` up to 50 times (`gate_grace` 10 s), then ran `gate_kill KILL`.
- `gate_kill` sent `kill -s "$_gks" -- "-$_gkg"`: the signal was a variable, so the guard
  could not see the KILL.
- `gate_reap` and `gate_alive` were on the guard's allow-list (`MARKED`) by name.

This slice moves `gate_end`. The Python watchdogs in `mfx.py`, `rtvar.py` and `report.py` are
slice 2.

## The change

**The guard.** `tests/check-bounded-run.sh` refuses a signal named through a variable:
`kill`, `pkill` or `killall` whose first argument is `-` and a parameter (`kill -"$sig"`,
`kill -$sig`), or with `-s`, `-n` or `--signal` and a parameter anywhere on the line
(`kill -s "$sig"`, `kill -s "${sig}"`, `--signal="$sig"`). It cannot trace what the variable
holds, so every such line outside the primitive is refused unless it carries a pinned marker.
A group after `--` (`kill -s TERM -- "-$pgid"`) is not taken for a signal. The two lines of
`bench_signal_group` carry pinned markers. Five mutants, one per spelling, are refused for this
rule in their own file.

Rework round 1 widened the rule to the spellings the review found it missed, each of which can
send KILL: a `SIG` prefix (`kill -SIG"$sig"`, `kill -SIG$sig`, `kill -s SIG"$sig"`), a backtick
substitution (``kill -`cmd` ``, ``kill -s `cmd` ``), and a first argument that is itself the
variable (`kill $flag "$p"`, `kill "$@"`, and so `kill "$pid"` too). The word is matched wherever
it stands, so `bench_signal_group KILL "$_sg_p"` in `bench_stop_groups` is refused as well and
carries a pinned marker. Seven more mutants, one per new spelling, are refused for this rule in
their own file.

**The primitive.** `bench_stop_groups <grace> <how> <pid>...` is the list form of
`bench_stop_group`: TERM and CONT to each group once, one grace for all of them, KILL to each
one left, up to 2 s for all to go, then the reap (none with `group`). `bench_stop_group` is now
that function for one pid, so the core still has one TERM-then-KILL. A stop of several groups
one after another would have given each a grace of its own, and the last its KILL up to
10 s per group later.

**The scheduler.** `scripts/gate-checks.sh` sources the primitive from `bench/core/` in its own
directory, and fails with status 2 before any check starts when it cannot read it.

- `gate_start` records each check with `bench_spawn_id`, so its group is signalled only while it
  is still that check's (FIX172). `gate_end` records a check that a signal kept from that record.
- `gate_end` is `bench_stop_groups "$gate_grace" group "$@"`.
- `gate_reap` tests a check with `bench_pid_alive`. Once it has stopped what the check left, it
  takes the check out of the running list (`gate_drop`), and only then forgets its record. So a
  signal between the two never has `gate_cleanup` record that pid anew, as whatever holds it by
  then, and signal it.
- `gate_kill` and `gate_alive` are gone, and so are both `gate-checks.sh` entries in `MARKED`.
  The four marker mutants that used `gate_alive`'s marker now use `lock.sh`'s.
- A standard error that is closed becomes `/dev/null` before the primitive is sourced, as in
  `bench_spawn` and `scripts/test.sh`. `bench_stop_groups` copies it to fd 8 in its definition;
  with fd 2 closed that copy failed at every call, so the body never ran and no group was stopped,
  silently (`gate_end`'s `|| :`). `scripts/ci-checks.sh` `exec`s the scheduler as it was started,
  so a run with its standard error closed reaches it.

What stays: TERM first, a grace of 10 s (`gate_grace`, passed to the primitive, so
`BENCH_STOP_GRACE` does not change it), then KILL; every process in each group ended; the
statuses 129, 130 and 143; a group is alive while it has a member.

Behaviour changes, both from the primitive, as every stop through it behaves:

- Each group gets a CONT right after its TERM, so a stopped member acts on the TERM at once
  instead of waiting for the KILL.
- After the KILL the stop waits up to 2 s for the groups to go. Main's `gate_end` returned right
  after its KILL. This delays the end of a stop that needs the KILL by 2 s at most.

What a test cannot stage: the forget-then-signal window needs a signal to land between two
statements of `gate_reap` and a pid recycled, in that time, by a process that leads its own group.
Neither can be held from outside without changing the scheduler, so no case covers it.

# Slice 2: the Python watchdogs stop process groups TERM first

## What happened

`tests/check-bounded-run.sh` allowed three Python TERM-then-KILL copies as pinned, marked lines,
all in `scripts/metalfx-report/mfx.py`: `stop_group` (`killpg` TERM, a wait, then `killpg`
SIGKILL) and `run_engine`'s pty watchdog (`terminate`, 5 s, `p.kill()`, and `p.kill()` at once on
an interrupt), which signalled the engine's pid alone. And it did not see
`subprocess.run(..., timeout=)`, which on the timeout (or on any exception) sends SIGKILL to the
direct child only: `parse_scenario` in `mfx.py`, the selftest's `rt-variations.sh run` in
`scripts/pins/rtvar.py`, and `sysinfo` and `engine_run` in `scripts/upscale-report/report.py`.

## The change

**The helper.** `scripts/lib/bounded.py`, standard library only:

- `spawn(cmd, **popen_kw) -> Child`: the command in a session (and so a process group) of its
  own, its identity (pid, and its `ps -o lstart=` start time in UTC) read at once. SIGINT,
  SIGTERM and SIGHUP are held from before the fork until it is recorded (the child starts with the
  caller's mask), so a signal in that window still stops it.
- `signal_group(child, sig)`: only while the group is still the child's. Until the child is
  reaped its pid, and so its group id, cannot go to another process; a start time other than the
  one read at spawn means the pid was reaped behind Popen's back, and with no start time read at
  spawn the unreaped pid is taken as the child's. After the reap a group with its id is the
  child's only while no process has the pid (no new process gets the id of a process group that
  has a member), whatever ps says: the reap is tested first, so a ps that fails cannot make a
  process that took the pid the child. `ps` has `PS_WAIT` (5 s) to answer; past it, it is
  stopped and its answer is "unknown" (the child's while unreaped, not the child's once reaped).
- A group whose only member is the unreaped, exited child (a zombie) cannot be signalled on
  macOS: `killpg` fails with `EPERM`. `stop` then sends nothing, does not reap and returns False.
  `run` reads the output after the stop, which reaps the child, so a timeout's `Result` has the
  child's status all the same.
- `stop(child, grace=10, pump=None)`: TERM and CONT to the group, up to the grace for it to go,
  KILL to what is left, up to 2 s, then the reap; SIGINT, SIGTERM and SIGHUP held meanwhile. When
  the TERM is not sent (nothing is left, or the group is not the child's) nothing more is sent.
  `pump(t)` reads the command's output on each poll.
- `run(cmd, timeout=None, grace=10, **popen_kw) -> Result`: subprocess.run's shape. `Result.how`
  is `exit`, `signal` (`Result.signal` its number) or `timeout`. On a timeout the pipes are read
  once after the stop (up to 2 s), so `stdout` and `stderr` hold what the command wrote; they are
  None only when a process it left behind still holds them open. On any exception (Ctrl-C,
  SystemExit) the group is stopped and the exception goes on; while it runs, a SIGTERM or SIGHUP
  whose handler is the default raises `SystemExit(128 + signal)`, so the caller's own signal stops
  the group too.

**The callers.** None is kept.

- `mfx.py`: `stop_group` is gone. `sh_group` is `bounded.run` with `lock_stop_wait` as its grace
  and still raises `TimeoutExpired`, now once the group is stopped. `run_engine` starts the engine
  with `bounded.spawn` and stops it with `bounded.stop` (`ENGINE_GRACE`, 5 s, the pty read
  meanwhile) when its line matched, on its timeout and on an interrupt. `parse_scenario` reads
  through `bounded.run`. `sh()` lost its `timeout` parameter, which no caller passed.
  `run_engine`'s `finally` closes the pty and writes the log even when that stop raises a signal
  it held.
- `rtvar.py`: the selftest's run is `bounded.run`; its tree copy holds `scripts/lib/bounded.py`.
- `report.py`: `sysinfo` and `engine_run` are `bounded.run`.

**What changes for them.**

- The grace. `parse_scenario`, `sysinfo`, `engine_run` and the rtvar selftest's run used to KILL
  the direct child at once on their timeout. Now the group gets TERM, up to 10 s (`GRACE`), KILL,
  and up to 2 s (`AFTER_KILL`) for it to go, so a timeout can cost each up to about 12 s more
  (`sysinfo`'s 10 s, up to about 24 s). `sh_group` keeps `lock_stop_wait` as its grace, and
  `run_engine` 5 s.
- The engine's session. `run_engine` used to start the engine in mfx's own process group; now it
  is in a session of its own. A TERM, HUP or INT to mfx still stops it (mfx's handler raises
  SystemExit, and `run_engine`'s `finally` stops the engine's group). A SIGKILL to mfx's process
  group no longer reaches it: the engine, which does not quit after a demo, then runs on with no
  bound. The engine gets no controlling terminal (the pty's slave is inherited, not opened), so
  closing the master sends it no SIGHUP.

**The guard.** A new Python rule refuses `subprocess.run`, `call`, `check_call` and `check_output`
with a `timeout=` on the logical line. The three `mfx.py` entries leave `MARKED`; the helper's KILL
(`signal_group(c, signal.SIGKILL)` in `stop`) is the one pinned Python marker. What it does not
see: a subprocess function imported under another name, and the `Popen.kill()` that
subprocess.run's own cleanup sends on an exception in a run with no timeout (`sh()`, `uptime`).

What a test cannot stage: a recycled pid. The probe tests the identity check directly instead.
