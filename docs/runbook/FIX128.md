# FIX128: check-bench's lock give-up case waits for a state, not a time

On 2026-11-18 a full gate run beside a second full gate failed `tests/check-bench.sh` with:

```
check-bench: give-up: the waiter showed no ticket (its pid= and label=) while it waited
check-bench: failed: t_lock_giveup
```

The lock code was correct. The test was not.

## The cause

`t_lock_giveup` started its waiter G with `BENCH_LOCK_WAIT=1` and polled for G's ticket for as
long as G ran. The lock reads whole seconds (`date +%s`): it takes `t0` before the enqueue and
gives up at the first check where `now - t0 >= 1`. When a second boundary fell between `t0` and
that first check, G gave up straight away, so its ticket existed only between the enqueue and
that check, a few milliseconds. The test's poll (an `ls`, a `grep` and a `ps` per round) missed
it. The longer a loaded host takes between the two reads, the likelier a boundary falls between
them. This is FIX111's whole-second edge again, in another case. The lock keeps its contract
("for at most `BENCH_LOCK_WAIT` seconds"), so `lock.sh` is unchanged.

## The red

Two sets of 20 runs of `sh tests/check-bench.sh t_lock_giveup` on the unfixed test, each beside
16 `yes > /dev/null` busy loops started and killed by pid inside one heavy.sh hold (12 cores, load
average 45.7 to 58.3):

- the real clock: passed 20 of 20. Load alone did not hit the window in 20 runs;
- a fake `date` on PATH whose `+%s` reads one second ahead once a waiter labelled G has a ticket
  (a second boundary just after the enqueue, which is what load makes likelier): failed 4 of 20
  (runs 2, 9, 12 and 19), each with the message above, as in the gate.

## The fix

The test now gives G a clock of its own. `clockshim` writes `$E/clock/date`, whose `+%s` reads
a fixed time until `$E/tick` exists and an hour later after that. Every other form runs the real
`date`. G runs with that directory first on its PATH, so it cannot give up before the test has
seen its ticket, however slow the host. The test polls for the ticket (`pid=` and `label=G`)
while G runs, for 300 polls of 0.1 s (at least 30 s). Then it checks that G is still waiting and
creates `$E/tick`, and G gives up at its next check (within its 1 s poll). The bound only catches
a waiter that never queues. The other cases give a waiter 10 s to say it waits, and 30 s leaves
room for a host loaded harder than the gate's. The rest of the case (exit 75, the holder named,
no ticket left, the TERM waiter) is unchanged.

## The other cases that sample a lock state after a sleep

- `t_lock_stall`: fixed. Its maker stalled a fixed 2.5 s between its mkdir and its holder write,
  and the taker (grace 1 s, a whole-second edge of up to 2 s) had to start, reclaim and hold within
  that time. A loaded host can outlast 2.5 s, and then the maker never backs off. Now `stall`
  injects a stall that lasts until `$E/unstall` exists (60 s at most), the test creates it once
  `wait_held 30 taker` sees the taker hold, and the taker holds until `$E/release` instead of for
  3.5 s. The poll for the maker's "waiting for the lock held by taker" goes from 8 s to 30 s.
- `t_lock_stall_place`: fixed the same way. The stall was a fixed 6 s, which had to outlast C's
  reclaim and W's queueing before `N backed off before W queued` was judged. `unstall` is now
  created after that check, `wait_held` for C goes from 5 s to 30 s, and the poll for N's wait
  goes from 10 s to 30 s.
- `t_lock_giveup`, the TERM waiter T: safe. `wait_said` waits for T's note, and T writes its
  ticket before the note. It then has `BENCH_LOCK_WAIT=60`, and H holds until the test releases
  it, so the ticket cannot go before T is TERMed.
- `t_lock_dead_waiter` (W1 first), `t_lock_queue_reentry`, `t_lock_queue_status`,
  `t_lock_ticket_back`, `t_lock_zombie_waiter` (Z's ticket and status): safe. Each samples once,
  after `wait_said`, waiters that have the default wait (3600 s) behind a holder that keeps the
  lock until the test releases it. A dead waiter's ticket is removed only by another waiter's
  poll, and none runs at the sample.
- `t_lock_zombie_waiter`, `kill -0` on the zombie: safe by a wide margin. Its perl parent reaps
  it after 30 s at the latest, and the check comes a few seconds after the spawn.
- The latency bounds (`d <= 3.1` in `t_lock_dead_waiter` and `t_lock_zombie_waiter`) and
  `t_lock_fifo`'s poll of 3 s time a hand-over rather than sample a state. They passed in every
  loaded run here and are left as they are.
