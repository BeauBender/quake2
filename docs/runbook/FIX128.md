# FIX128: check-bench's lock give-up case waits for a state, not a time

On 2026-11-18 a full gate run beside a second full gate failed `tests/check-bench.sh` with:

```
check-bench: give-up: the waiter showed no ticket (its pid= and label=) while it waited
check-bench: failed: t_lock_giveup
```

The lock code was correct. The test was not.

## The cause

`t_lock_giveup` started its waiter G with `BENCH_LOCK_WAIT=1` and polled for G's ticket for as
long as G ran. The lock reads whole seconds (`date +%s`): it takes `t0` before the enqueue and
gives up at the first check where `now - t0 >= 1`. When a second boundary fell between `t0` and
that first check, G gave up straight away, so its ticket existed only between the enqueue and
that check, a few milliseconds. The test's poll (an `ls`, a `grep` and a `ps` per round) missed
it. The longer a loaded host takes between the two reads, the likelier a boundary falls between
them. This is FIX111's whole-second edge again, in another case. The lock keeps its contract
("for at most `BENCH_LOCK_WAIT` seconds"), so `lock.sh` is unchanged.

## The red

Two sets of 20 runs of `sh tests/check-bench.sh t_lock_giveup` on the unfixed test, each beside
16 `yes > /dev/null` busy loops started and killed by pid inside one heavy.sh hold (12 cores, load
average 45.7 to 58.3):

- the real clock: passed 20 of 20. Load alone did not hit the window in 20 runs;
- a fake `date` on PATH whose `+%s` reads one second ahead once a waiter labelled G has a ticket
  (a second boundary just after the enqueue, which is what load makes likelier): failed 4 of 20
  (runs 2, 9, 12 and 19), each with the message above, as in the gate.

## The fix

The test now gives G a clock of its own. `clockshim 1` writes `$E/clock/date`, whose `+%s`
reads a fixed time until `$E/tick` exists and exactly 1 s later (G's `BENCH_LOCK_WAIT`) after
that. Every other form runs `/bin/date` (the `date` on PATH only where there is none), with its
path quoted in the shim. G runs with that directory first on its PATH, so it cannot give up
before the test has seen its ticket, however slow the host. The test polls for the ticket (`pid=` and `label=G`)
while G runs, for 300 polls of 0.1 s (at least 30 s). Then it checks that G is still waiting and
creates `$E/tick`, and G gives up at its next check (within its 1 s poll). The bound only catches
a waiter that never queues. The other cases give a waiter 10 s to say it waits, and 30 s leaves
room for a host loaded harder than the gate's. The rest of the case (exit 75, the holder named,
no ticket left, the TERM waiter) is unchanged.

## The other cases that sample a lock state after a sleep

- `t_lock_stall`: fixed. Its maker stalled a fixed 2.5 s between its mkdir and its holder write,
  and the taker (grace 1 s, a whole-second edge of up to 2 s) had to start, reclaim and hold within
  that time. A loaded host can outlast 2.5 s, and then the maker never backs off. Now `stall`
  injects a stall that lasts until `$E/unstall` exists (60 s at most), the test creates it once
  `wait_held 30 taker` sees the taker hold, and the taker holds until `$E/release` (120 s at
  most) instead of for 3.5 s. The poll for the maker's "waiting for the lock held by taker" goes
  from 8 s to 30 s. The maker and the taker wait 300 s (review round 1, below).
- `t_lock_stall_place`: fixed the same way. The stall was a fixed 6 s, which had to outlast C's
  reclaim and W's queueing before `N backed off before W queued` was judged. `unstall` is now
  created after that check, `wait_held` for C goes from 5 s to 30 s, the poll for N's wait goes
  from 10 s to 30 s, and the case's waiters wait 300 s instead of 30 s.
- `t_lock_giveup`, the TERM waiter T: safe. `wait_said` waits for T's note, and T writes its
  ticket before the note. It then has `BENCH_LOCK_WAIT=60`, and H holds until the test releases
  it, so the ticket cannot go before T is TERMed.
- `t_lock_queue_reentry`: fixed. It compared the nested call's list of tickets with W's ticket,
  but read W's ticket only after the nested call had finished. By then H could release and W
  could take the lock, and its ticket was gone. The first full run of the fixed `check-bench.sh`
  under load failed here (`re-entry: the nested call took a ticket: w.<W's key>`). W's ticket is
  now read once, while H still holds, and compared from there.
- `t_lock_dead_waiter` (W1 first), `t_lock_queue_status`, `t_lock_ticket_back`,
  `t_lock_zombie_waiter` (Z's ticket and status) and `t_lock_own_pid`: fixed in review round 1.
  Each samples once, after `wait_said`, waiters behind a holder that keeps the lock until the
  test releases it, and a dead waiter's ticket is removed only by another waiter's poll, none of
  which runs at the sample. But the waiters did not have the default wait (3600 s), as the first
  version of this audit said: they had `fresh`'s 10 s (`t_lock_own_pid`'s W 5 s), shorter than
  the case's own bounds, so a slow host could make a waiter give up first. Each now waits 300 s.
  The table below gives, for each waiter, the longest it can wait while every one of its case's
  polls stays within its bound: the sum of those bounds from its start to its own `finish` (or
  `wait_gone`, or the kill).

| case | waiter | longest wait the case's bounds allow | wait before | now |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `t_lock_stall` | maker | 5 + 30 + 30 + 20 = 85 s (the poll for the lock directory, `wait_held`, the poll for its wait, `wait_gone`) | 10 s | 300 s |
| `t_lock_stall` | taker | 30 s (`wait_held`) | 10 s | 300 s |
| `t_lock_stall_place` | N | 5 + 30 + 10 + 30 + 30 + 30 = 135 s (the same, `wait_said`, `finish` C, its own `finish`) | 30 s | 300 s |
| `t_lock_stall_place` | W | 10 + 30 + 30 + 30 + 30 = 130 s | 30 s | 300 s |
| `t_lock_dead_waiter` | W1, W2 | 10 + 10 = 20 s (to the kill); 10 + 30 = 40 s | 10 s | 300 s |
| `t_lock_queue_status` | S1, S2, S3 | 60 s, 80 s, 10 + 3 × 30 = 100 s | 10 s | 300 s |
| `t_lock_ticket_back` | W1, W2 | 10 + 10 + 5 + 30 = 55 s; 10 + 5 + 30 + 30 = 75 s | 10 s | 300 s |
| `t_lock_zombie_waiter` | Z, W | 10 s (to the kill); 10 + 30 = 40 s | 10 s | 300 s |
| `t_lock_own_pid` | W | 10 + 30 = 40 s (`wait_said`, `finish`) | 5 s | 300 s |
| `t_lock_queue_reentry` | W | 10 + 10 + 30 + 30 = 80 s (`wait_said`, the nested call, `finish` H, its own `finish`) | 10 s | 300 s |

The longest is 135 s, so 300 s leaves every case's stated polls as its binding bounds, with more
than twice the margin. A waiter that does outlive its case's bound is reaped (`finish`, and now
`t_lock_stall`'s `wait_gone`), so the longer wait never holds the test up.
- `t_lock_zombie_waiter`, `kill -0` on the zombie: safe by a wide margin. Its perl parent reaps
  it after 30 s at the latest, and the check comes a few seconds after the spawn.
- The latency bounds (`d <= 3.1` in `t_lock_dead_waiter` and `t_lock_zombie_waiter`) and
  `t_lock_fifo`'s poll of 3 s time a hand-over rather than sample a state. They passed in every
  loaded run here and are left as they are.

## Mutants

The mutant table in `tests/check-bench.sh` is unchanged. In the full run under load (below) it
caught every mutant, among them the three on the changed cases:

- `queue-ticket-left`: caught by `t_lock_giveup` (`a waiter that gave up left its ticket`);
- `holder-replaced`: caught by `t_lock_stall` (`the maker that backed off does not wait for the
  taker: ... held by a holder that has not written its holder file yet`);
- `queue-backoff-new-key`: caught by `t_lock_stall_place` (`a maker that backed off lost its
  place: C W N`).

Each new wait also has a case of its own. Each mutant below is one change to the line the wait
covers, run on a copy of the tree with `sh tests/check-bench.sh <case>`:

| mutant | change | caught by | how |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| giveup-early | the give-up check `-ge "$_la_wait"` reads `-ge 0` | `t_lock_giveup`, 12.5 s | the waiter gave up before its ticket was seen |
| giveup-never | the give-up check reads `if false` | `t_lock_giveup`, 35.1 s | the waiter did not finish once the clock moved on |
| ticket-no-label | the ticket's `label=` is written empty | `t_lock_giveup`, 36.9 s | the 30 s poll for the ticket ran out |
| holder-replaced | the holder `ln` reads `mv -f` | `t_lock_stall`, 9.9 s | the maker that backed off waits for a holder with no holder file |
| backoff-new-key | a maker that backs off takes a new key | `t_lock_stall_place`, 1.9 s | the order is `C W N` |
| reentry-ticket | a nested call under the holder's token also enqueues | `t_lock_queue_reentry`, 0.9 s | the nested call saw two tickets |

`giveup-ticket-left` (only the give-up's `bench_lock_unqueue` dropped) passes, on the old test
as on the new one: the EXIT trap still takes the ticket out. That is why the table's
`queue-ticket-left` drops both.

## Under load

Each set ran beside 16 `yes > /dev/null` busy loops on the 12-core M6, started and killed by pid
inside the same heavy.sh hold. The load average is the 1-minute figure from `uptime` after each
run.

- `t_lock_giveup` with the clock-jump fake on PATH, which failed 4 of 20 before the fix: passed
  20 of 20 (load average 8.7 to 20.1).
- `t_lock_giveup t_lock_stall t_lock_stall_place t_lock_queue_reentry`: passed 20 of 20 in a row
  (load average 25.0 to 33.1).
- The full `sh tests/check-bench.sh`, every case and the whole mutant table: `check-bench: ok`
  (load average 33.1 at the start and 32.5 at the end).

Then, in the same hold, `tests/check-bench-quake2.sh` passed (`ok (32 cases)`), and so did
`make test` (`yq2test: 382 tests, 0 failed`).
