# FIX171: the test cleanups signal only a process that is still the one they recorded

## What happened

While gate runs were going in other worktrees, a CI-wait script's `perl` sleep got SIGTERM: its shell
printed `Terminated: 15` for it and lived on. The shell traps nothing, and `perl` leads no process
group of its own, so the signal was a TERM to that one PID, not to a group.

macOS hands out PIDs in sequence and reuses a freed one once the counter laps back to it. Under the
load of a gate run that takes 15 to 30 minutes, less with several gates running (during this fix's
probes, PIDs went from about 79000 to 99000 in under a minute). `/bin/sh` here is bash, which collects an
exited child from its SIGCHLD handler at once, so even an unwaited `$!` does not keep its PID.

## The finding

The audit of every signal site in the gate's checks (phase 1) put `tests/check-bench.sh`'s `cleanup`
first. It sent TERM to every PID on its list, `$tmp/pids`. The list held each `start` child and three
grandchildren read from files (the lock shell from the holder file, the INT test's `_h`, the
reclaim guard's group member), was never trimmed, and was checked only with `pid_running` (the PID
exists and is not a zombie). The list lives for the whole run, minutes under a loaded gate, so by
cleanup a recorded PID can belong to an unrelated process. This fits the evidence, though the
victim's PID was not captured, so it is the likely source, not a proven one.

The audit found the same class, with shorter windows, in:

- `tests/check-bench-entry.sh` `cleanup` (its `forget` already trimmed the list);
- `tests/check-metalfx-report.sh`: a `kill -KILL $pid` after the `wait` that collected `$pid`, and
  TERM2's second TERM to a PID read from a file;
- `scripts/ios-sim.sh` selftest: the KILL of the PID in `hang.pid`, checked only with `kill -0`.

The process-group signals in these files were already safe (`stray_stop` checks the command line,
the group and that the group is not the check's own; `check-bench.sh`'s guard test signals the group
only while it still has its member).

## The fix

**The primitive** (the same functions in `tests/check-bench.sh` and `tests/check-bench-entry.sh`):

- `pid_id <pid>` is a checksum of `ps -o lstart=` (the start time, which exec does not change) and
  `ps -o args=` (the command line). It fails when the PID does not run or `ps` gives no answer for
  either.
- `record <pid>` puts the PID on the list with its identity at that moment. A PID that does not run
  is not put on it. The list holds `<pid> <identity>` lines.
- `pid_same <pid> <identity>` is true only while the PID runs with that identity. `stop_listed <list>`
  sends TERM only to the PIDs that pass it, and `cleanup` calls it. A PID that has gone, or that `ps`
  gives no answer for, gets no signal.
- `settle <pid>` runs before a `$!` child is recorded. It waits, at most 1 s, until the child is no
  longer showing this shell's command line (a fork has it until its exec) or `env`'s (env execs the
  command next), so the identity recorded is the command's. On the first read it has almost always
  exec'd already.

**`tests/check-bench.sh`:**

- `start` settles and records its child.
- The three grandchildren read from files are recorded with their identity when they are read.
- A PID comes off the list once it has been waited for or seen gone. `wait_gone` forgets a PID it
  sees gone. `collect` (wait, then forget) replaces every `wait` on a PID. The lock shell is forgotten
  once its wrapper has written its end. The group member is waited for after the TERM to its group.
- New test `t_cleanup_id` puts its own live processes on a list with identities that do not match:
  another process's, its own command line with another start time, and its own start time with
  another command line. It also adds lines that name no PID. All of them survive `stop_listed`. The
  one listed with its own identity is stopped.

**`tests/check-bench-entry.sh`:**

- `run`, and the `$!` children of `t_nested`, `t_kgrp` and `t_grace`, settle and record. The orphan's
  PID read from `run.pid` is recorded when it is read. `forget` matches the PID field.
- `stray_stop <pid> <start> <text> [<group>]` keeps its command-line and group checks. It also needs
  the start time that `pid_start` read along with the PID (the `start` line, the make line, `run.pid`).
- `t_cleanup_id` does the same as in `check-bench.sh`, and checks that `stray_stop` with another start
  time leaves its process running.
- The end-of-run check that the list is empty prints only the PIDs.

**`tests/check-metalfx-report.sh`:**

- The KILL after the collecting `wait` is gone. When the stub never started, the run is KILLed only
  while it answers `kill -0` and is then waited for. The wait for the stub's child now also ends when
  the run is gone.
- TERM2 reads the generator's `ps -o lstart= -o args=` before the first TERM. It sends the second only
  while that is unchanged.

**`scripts/ios-sim.sh`:** the fake `xcrun`'s hung calls go through `hang <seconds>`. That writes
`sleep <seconds>.<pid>`, a command line unique to the PID, to `hang.cmd`, then writes `hang.pid`, then
execs that command, as the fake launch already does with `app.cmd`. The two selftest KILLs of the PID
in `hang.pid` first compare its `ps -o command=` with `hang.cmd`.

## Not changed

- **The bench core.** `scripts/bench/core/` (`bench_signal_group`'s PID fallback, and `bench_watch`,
  which identifies the lock child only by `kill -0` and `ps` stat) is a separate card.
- **`reap`, in both check-bench files.** It TERMs and then KILLs a `$!` child that `wait_gone` has just
  seen running. Its KILL follows a `wait_gone` that ended with the PID still running.
- **The direct signals to a `$!` child in the tests.** These target a child the test has just seen
  running.
- **`tests/check-metalfx-report.sh`'s KILLs of the stub's PIDs read from files** (`cpid` in e2e-group,
  `spid` and `cpid` in e2e-lock). The audit did not list them. They are KILLs a second or more after
  the read, with a `kill -0` check. If they needed an identity check, the "left running" failures
  that follow them could be missed when the stub execs after writing its PID.
- **`tests/check-stop-invariants.sh` and `scripts/pins.sh`.** The card named them as leads. They are
  outside this phase's scope.

## Proof

**Red, before the fix.** `cleanup`'s loop as on `origin/main` (`pid_running` only) TERMs an unrelated
`sleep` put on its list with an identity that is not its own. The probe printed `Terminated: 15` for
it, the line the card reported. With the old `stop_listed` (or `stray_stop` without its start time),
both new `t_cleanup_id` tests fail on every non-matching case.

it where the site reads its target, and ends it afterwards:
