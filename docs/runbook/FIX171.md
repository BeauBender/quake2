# FIX171: the test cleanups signal only a process that is still the one they recorded

## What happened

While gate runs were going in other worktrees, a CI-wait script's `perl` sleep got SIGTERM: its shell
printed `Terminated: 15` for it and lived on. The shell traps nothing, and `perl` leads no process
group of its own, so the signal was a TERM to that one PID, not to a group.

macOS hands out PIDs in sequence and reuses a freed one once the counter laps back to it. Under the
load of a gate run that takes 15 to 30 minutes, less with several gates running (during this fix's
probes, PIDs went from about 79000 to 99000 in under a minute). `/bin/sh` here is bash, which collects an
exited child from its SIGCHLD handler at once, so even an unwaited `$!` does not keep its PID.

## The finding

The audit of every signal site in the gate's checks (phase 1) put `tests/check-bench.sh`'s `cleanup`
first. It sent TERM to every PID on its list, `$tmp/pids`. The list held each `start` child and three
grandchildren read from files (the lock shell from the holder file, the INT test's `_h`, the
reclaim guard's group member), was never trimmed, and was checked only with `pid_running` (the PID
exists and is not a zombie). The list lives for the whole run, minutes under a loaded gate, so by
cleanup a recorded PID can belong to an unrelated process. This fits the evidence, though the
victim's PID was not captured, so it is the likely source, not a proven one.

The audit found the same class, with shorter windows, in:

- `tests/check-bench-entry.sh` `cleanup` (its `forget` already trimmed the list);
- `tests/check-metalfx-report.sh`: a `kill -KILL $pid` after the `wait` that collected `$pid`, and
  TERM2's second TERM to a PID read from a file;
- `scripts/ios-sim.sh` selftest: the KILL of the PID in `hang.pid`, checked only with `kill -0`.

The process-group signals in these files were already safe (`stray_stop` checks the command line,
the group and that the group is not the check's own; `check-bench.sh`'s guard test signals the group
only while it still has its member).

## The fix

**The primitive** (the same functions in `tests/check-bench.sh` and `tests/check-bench-entry.sh`):

- `pid_id <pid>` is a checksum of `ps -o lstart=`, the start time, which exec does not change. It
  fails when the PID does not run or `ps` gives no answer.
- The command line is not part of the identity. It guards against no PID reuse that the start time
  does not already cover: another process in the PID with the same start second would need the PID
  counter to go round within that second. It did make cleanup skip the check's own children once they
  exec'd: `bench.sh baseline put` re-execs under the lock, and so does `scripts/test.sh`. Round 1 of
  this fix had it in, and a review probe showed 6 of 6 such children left running by `stop_listed`.
- `record <pid>` puts the PID on the list with its identity at that moment. A PID that has already
  ended, or that `ps` gives no answer for, is put on it with an empty identity. The list holds
  `<pid> <identity>` lines.
- `pid_same <pid> <identity>` is true only while the PID runs with that identity, and never for an
  empty identity. `stop_listed <list>` sends TERM only to the PIDs that pass it, and `cleanup` calls
  it. A PID that has gone, or that `ps` gives no answer for, gets no signal. A PID listed with no
  identity is never signalled. It stays on the list, so check-bench-entry's end-of-run check that the
  list is empty still sees a test that did not forget it.
- `settle <pid>` runs before a `$!` child is recorded. It waits, at most 1 s, until the child is no
  longer showing this shell's command line (a fork has it until its exec) or `env`'s (env execs the
  command next), so that a signal to it reaches the command and not the forked shell. The identity
  does not depend on it.

**`tests/check-bench.sh`:**

- `start` settles and records its child.
- The three grandchildren read from files are recorded with their identity when they are read.
- The two PIDs signalled straight from a file, the quiet probe's grandchild (`gc.quiet`) and the
  zombie waiter's `Z` (`z.pid`), are read with their start time and signalled only while they still
  have it.
- A PID comes off the list once it has been waited for or seen gone. `wait_gone` forgets a PID it
  sees gone. `collect` (wait, then forget) replaces every `wait` on a PID. The lock shell is forgotten
  once its wrapper has written its end. The group member is waited for after the TERM to its group.
- New test `t_cleanup_id` puts its own live processes on a list with identities that do not match:
  another process's (PID 1's start time) and its own command line with another start time. It also
  adds lines that name no PID, and a PID recorded after it ended, which must be listed with no
  identity. All of these survive `stop_listed`. The one listed with its own identity is stopped. So is
  one recorded before it exec'd another program: the same start time with another command line is
  still the check's own. Its processes are shells that end on TERM, and by themselves within about
  30 s, and every wait on them is bounded, so a check started with TERM or PIPE ignored cannot stall
  there.

**`tests/check-bench-entry.sh`:**

- `run`, and the `$!` children of `t_nested`, `t_kgrp` and `t_grace`, settle and record. The orphan's
  PID read from `run.pid` is recorded when it is read. `forget` matches the PID field.
- `stray_stop <pid> <start> <text> [<group>]` keeps its command-line and group checks. It also needs
  the start time that `pid_start` read along with the PID (the `start` line, the make line, `run.pid`).
- `t_cleanup_id` does the same as in `check-bench.sh`, and checks that `stray_stop` with another start
  time leaves its process running. `stray_stop` keeps its command-line and group checks on top of the
  start time: it ends strays a failed test left, which it did not start, so it is stricter than the
  cleanup list.
- The end-of-run check that the list is empty prints only the PIDs.

**`tests/check-metalfx-report.sh`:**

- The KILL after the collecting `wait` is gone. When the stub never started, the run is KILLed only
  while it answers `kill -0` and is then waited for. The wait for the stub's child now also ends when
  the run is gone.
  The e2e-group case logs every `kill` and `wait` that names the run's PID. A signal after the wait
  that collected it fails the case.
- TERM2's two TERMs are `term_twice <pid>`. It reads the generator's `ps -o lstart= -o args=` before
  the first TERM and sends the second only while that is unchanged. If `ps` gave no answer, or the
  generator had changed, TERM2 now fails with that reason instead of skipping the second TERM
  silently. A new case runs `term_twice` on two processes the check starts. One execs another program
  on the first TERM, so it gets no second TERM and is still there. The other does not change, gets
  both, and ends on the second. Here the command line is part of the identity on purpose: the
  generator does not exec between the two TERMs, and an exec is the only change a test can make.
- The KILL of the second run (`$p2`) after its TERM loop, and the TERM to the lock holder (`$hp`),
  whose `sleep 10` has ended by then on every passing run, go only to a PID that answers `kill -0`.

**`scripts/ios-sim.sh`:** the fake `xcrun`'s hung calls go through `hang <seconds>`. That writes
`sleep <seconds>.<pid>`, a command line unique to the PID, to `hang.cmd`, then writes `hang.pid`, then
execs that command, as the fake launch already does with `app.cmd`. The two selftest KILLs of the PID
in `hang.pid` first compare its `ps -o command=` with `hang.cmd`. A PID that runs but that `ps` gives
no command line for fails the case with that reason. For each of the two sites, a new case has the
fake start a decoy, a `sleep` in its own process group that `hang.pid` names but that runs another
command (`FAKE_SCENARIO=hangdecoy` for the check after the smoke, `FAKE_LAUNCHER=hang1decoy` for the
check after a signal). `st_decoy` fails if the decoy was killed, then stops it while it still runs its
own command.

## Not changed

This phase does not close the card. What is left is phase 3:

- **`reap`'s KILL, in both check-bench files.** `reap` TERMs a `$!` child, then KILLs it when
  `wait_gone` timed out with the PID still running. The KILL has no identity check of its own.
- **`tests/check-metalfx-report.sh`'s KILLs of the stub's PIDs read from files** (`cpid` in e2e-group,
  `spid` and `cpid` in e2e-lock). They are KILLs a second or more after the read, with only a
  `kill -0` check.
- **`tests/check-stop-invariants.sh` and `scripts/pins.sh`.** The card named them as leads.
- **The bench core, `scripts/bench/core/`** (`bench_signal_group`'s PID fallback, and `bench_watch`,
  which identifies the lock child only by `kill -0` and `ps` stat). This is FIX172.

The direct signals to a `$!` child that the test has just seen running are not in this list. They
target a child the test started and has not yet waited for.

## Proof

**Red, before the fix.** `cleanup`'s loop as on `origin/main` (`pid_running` only) TERMs an unrelated
`sleep` put on its list with an identity that is not its own. The probe printed `Terminated: 15` for
it, the line the card reported. With the old `stop_listed` (or `stray_stop` without its start time),
both new `t_cleanup_id` tests fail on every non-matching case.

**Disposition probes, after the fix.** Each probe uses a `sleep` that the probe itself started, puts
it where the site reads its target, and ends it afterwards:
