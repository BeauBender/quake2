# FIX211: Retro gets darker than stock as the output resolution rises

## What happened

TASK186 measured base1's start in the same view and found Retro (`r_presentation 2`) darker than
stock (`r_presentation 0`) above 640x480: 14.0 against 16.3 mean luma at 800x600, and 13.4
against 15.1 at 1920x1080. The 800x600 figure was from main, with the 480-line cap. Only the
1920x1080 figure came from a scratch build with the cap off. Each figure came from one run.

## The finding

There is no size-dependent darkening in Retro. The card's two low figures match, in value, the
alternate view below. That is an inference: those runs were not replayed. They were most likely
single runs that captured a different view of base1's start. The same different view turns up in stock too.

Across three runs of each look and size, 3 of the 18 stills came out 10 to 13 % darker. One was
Retro and two were stock:

| Look | Size | Mean luma (3 runs) | p90 (3 runs) |
|---|---|---|---|
| stock | 640x480 | 16.246, 16.252, 16.252 | 29, 29, 29 |
| Retro | 640x480 | **14.010**, 16.144, 16.144 | **26**, 28, 28 |
| stock | 800x600 | 16.266, 16.268, **14.081** | 29, 29, **26** |
| Retro | 800x600 | 16.219, 16.167, 16.167 | 29, 29, 29 |
| stock | 1920x1080 | 15.061, **13.524**, 15.132 | 25, **22**, 26 |
| Retro | 1920x1080 | 14.970, 14.970, 14.970 | 25, 25, 25 |

The dark stills are a different picture, not a dimmer copy of the same one. Against a normal run
of the same look and size, only 0.2 % of the dark still's pixels are unchanged (9 % at 640x480,
where Retro's dither repeats). On a 4 x 4 grid of the measured rows, the per-cell luma ratio runs
from 0.32 to 1.57. A scene lit more dimly would scale every cell by about the same amount.

Retro's dark 640x480 still and stock's dark 800x600 still have the same grid of ratios to within
0.04 per cell (1.37 1.18 0.57 0.32 on the top row). So it is one alternate view, and neither the
look nor the size produces it. In the engine log the dark run also creates one texture more than
the normal run (205 against 204 at teardown). So the capture's world frame 120 does not always
land on the same moment of the start.

Without the outliers, Retro is within 1.1 % of stock at every size:

| Size | Retro | Stock | Retro against stock |
|---|---|---|---|
| 640x480 | 16.144 | 16.246 to 16.252 | -0.6 % |
| 800x600 | 16.167 to 16.219 | 16.266 to 16.268 | -0.3 % to -0.6 % |
| 1920x1080 (main, the 480-line view enlarged) | 14.970 | 15.061 to 15.132 | -0.6 % to -1.1 % |
| 1920x1080 (scratch, no 480-line cap), two runs | 14.958, 14.958 | 15.061 to 15.132 | -0.7 % to -1.1 % |
| 800x600 (scratch, no 480-line cap), two runs | 16.169, 16.169 | 16.266 to 16.268 | -0.6 % |

So the card's goal already holds on main, and nothing in the Retro pass changes. The stock and
Retro pins are untouched, since no renderer source changed.

## The candidates, one at a time

Each candidate below is one scratch toggle, at the native view (480-line cap off) where the card
saw the gap. All numbers are mean luma. The reference runs are Retro at 800x600 with no cap,
16.169, and Retro at 1920x1080 with no cap, 14.958.

| Toggle | 800x600 | 1920x1080 | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| the 256-texel cap off | 16.255 | 14.952 | accounts for at most about 0.5 % at 800x600 and nothing at 1920x1080 |
| stock's texture filter in place of Retro's | 16.167 | 14.958 | ruled out: the filter is the same (`gl_texturemode` defaults to `GL_LINEAR_MIPMAP_NEAREST`, `r_anisotropic` to 0) |
| texels not quantized to RGB565 or ARGB4444 | 16.209 | 14.944 | ruled out |
| no Retro post-process (no RGB565 frame dither) | 16.293 | 15.080 | the pass costs 0.8 % at every size, 640x480 included (16.269 without it, 16.144 with it) |

With main's 480-line view, the same toggles at 800x600 give 16.155 (cap off), 16.152 (no texel
quantization) and 16.308 (no post-process). With `r_retro_filter 1`, Retro gives 16.197 at
800x600 and 16.252 at 640x480.

### The terms of the Retro pass that depend on the size

Read from `src/client/refresh/metal/`:

- **The 480-line view.** `mtl_retro_view_scale` (`common/mtl_retro_tex.h`) draws the 3D view at
  480 lines above that height. `rmb_gbuf_view_end` (`rmb_gbuf.m`) then enlarges it with a
  bilinear blit (`rm_fragment_blit`). The blit's weights are a linear average, so they keep the
  mean. Every Bayer rank's weight evens out over a 4 x 4 tile. Measured: 16.17 with the cap and
  16.17 without it at 800x600, and 14.97 against 14.96 at 1920x1080.
- **The 4 x 4 ordered dither and the 2 x 2 box filter** (`rm_fragment_retro` in `rm_shaders.metal`,
  `common/mtl_retro_math.h`). These are sized in pixels of the target the pass runs on: the
  480-line render target above 480 lines, otherwise the frame. The threshold `(r + 0.5) / 16`
  averages to one half over a tile at any size. Measured: -0.8 % at 640x480, 800x600 and
  1920x1080 alike.
- **Mip selection.** It follows the render size, the same as stock's. Retro's filter is stock's
  default (see above).
- **Not size-dependent:** the gamma curve (per pixel), and the texture cap and quantization (in
  texel space).

## How it was measured

- **Engine commit:** main `d93444d3`, built from a `git archive` extract. It carried a scratch
  patch that was never committed: a capture hook and five environment toggles, each off unless
  set. FIX218 (#825) is the card for a capture that shows the same view every time.
- **Still:** `quake2 +set basedir <game data> -portable` with the Metal renderer, then
  `+map base1`. The still is the frame at world frame 120, taken by the scratch hook from the
  rendered frame. `fixedtime 16667`, `r_hdr 0`, `r_metalfx 0` and every `r_rt_*` effect 0 are set
  on the command line, with `r_presentation 0` (stock) or `r_presentation 2` (Retro). Retro also
  sets `r_retro_filter 0` and `r_retro_gamma 0`. The size is the window size: 640x480, 800x600
  or 1920x1080. Each run is a new engine process with no `config.cfg`.
- **Luma:** read over the whole width and the rows from 8 % to 76 % of the height. Each pixel's
  luma is the Rec. 709 weighting of its 8-bit channels, 0.2126 R + 0.7152 G + 0.0722 B. The
  mean is over those pixels. The p90 is the value below which 90 % of those pixels fall. The pair
  comparison (unchanged pixels, and the 4 x 4 grid of luma ratios) is a scratch tool over the same
  rows. Nobody viewed the frames.
- **The five toggles**, each bypassing one rule of the renderer:
  - the 480-line cap not applied (the 3D view drawn at the window's own height);
  - the 256-texel cap not applied (large textures kept at full size);
  - texels not quantized to RGB565 or ARGB4444;
  - stock's texture filter used in place of Retro's;
  - the Retro post-process (the RGB565 frame dither) skipped.
- **Runs:** 35 engine runs, all under the host lock: 18 stills (three of each look at each size),
  4 no-cap references (two of each at 800x600 and 1920x1080), 8 toggle runs for the candidates
  table (four toggles at two sizes), 3 toggle runs at 800x600 with main's 480-line view, and 2
  runs with `r_retro_filter 1`.

## What is left

The capture method is the problem: world frame 120 of a `+map base1` start is not always the same
view. A Retro against stock comparison from one run of each can be off by 10 to 13 %. A later
comparison should take three or more runs. It should drop a still whose picture differs from the
others: a cell of the 4 x 4 luma-ratio grid more than 15 % off. Two normal runs of stock at 640x480
leave only 11 % of their pixels unchanged, yet no cell moves by more than 7 %, so counting
unchanged pixels is not the test. A comparison could also capture a demo frame instead of a live
start. This is reported to the card, not changed here.
