# FIX193: a TERM can no longer drop the engine's line from `run_engine`'s log

## What happened

The `free` leg of FIX186's case `engine-held-log` in `tests/check-metalfx-report.sh` failed often:

```
engine-held-log (free: no log, or not the engine's line, after a held TERM: exit 143)
```

The status was the TERM's, but the log lacked the fake engine's `engine-ready`. FIX191's correctness review saw
it in 11 of 16 runs of a driver that mirrors the leg, and on the first run of the real check. Every lost run's
first `SystemExit` was raised at the main loop's `os.read` in `scripts/metalfx-report/mfx.py`'s `run_engine`.

## The trace

The fake engine echoes `engine-ready` and at once sends the generator a TERM, whose handler raises
`SystemExit`. When that TERM is pending as `data = os.read(master, 65536)` returns, Python runs the handler
before `data` is appended to `buf`. The bytes have already left the pty, so the drain in the `finally` finds
nothing, and the log is written without the line.

The staged reproduction, in a scratch copy of the head (`git archive` of `scripts/` into a fresh directory),
with `mfx.py` unchanged:

- The fake engine writes `engine-ready`, waits for a `.go` file, TERMs its parent (the generator) and makes
  `.sent`.
- A driver replaces `mfx.os` with a copy of `os` whose `read`, on its first read with data, makes `.go` and
  waits for `.sent`. That wait sits between the read and the append, and it ends on state, not on a sleep.
- The driver prints each `SystemExit` in the `__context__` chain, frame by frame.

Three runs out of three lost the line, each with the same frames:

```
exit 143
SystemExit 0: <module>:41 > run_engine:763 > stop:198 > pthread_sigmask:60 > h:17
SystemExit 1: run_engine:732 > staged_read:31 > h:17
log: 'Terminated: 15\n'
```

The first TERM (`SystemExit 1`) is raised at `run_engine:732`, the main loop's read, inside the staged wait.
The second TERM, which the fake engine's trap sends when the `finally`'s stop TERMs its group, is held by
`bounded.stop`'s mask and raised as the stop ends (`stop:198`), before the drain; it does not cause the loss.

## The change

`run_engine` reads the pty through one helper, `take()`. It holds `bounded.STOP_SIGNALS` blocked around the
read and the append, the `pthread_sigmask` pattern `bounded.stop` uses around its pump, and restores the mask
in a `finally`. All three reads use it: the main loop, the leftover loop after the engine exits, and the drain
in the `finally`. A signal held during a read is raised as the mask is restored, once the bytes are in `buf`.
The same driver on the fixed code keeps the line in every run:

```
SystemExit 1: run_engine:744 > take:728 > pthread_sigmask:60 > h:17
log: 'engine-ready\nTerminated: 15\n'
```

The leftover loop's `select` now waits at most the time left to its deadline. So the two reads after the engine
exits take at most `2 * ENGINE_DRAIN`, as the comment on `ENGINE_DRAIN` says: no `select` in them waits past its
deadline, and a read after a `select` that found data does not block ([FIX191](FIX191.md)'s review, note N2).

## The proof

- **The pinned leg.** `engine-held-log` has a third leg, `read`, which is the staged reproduction above. It
  lands the TERM between the first read and its append in every run, the way `hold` lands it in `spawn`'s
  hold window. If the TERM is never sent, the leg fails with "the TERM never landed in its window".
- **Red, then green.** On FIX191's head the leg failed:
  `engine-held-log (read: no log, or not the engine's line, after a held TERM: exit 143)`. With the change, all
  three legs pass.
- **The mutant.** Mutant `engine-read-unheld` replaces the main loop's `take()` with an unheld
  `buf.extend(os.read(master, 65536))`, and the `read` leg kills it. The staged driver on the mutant loses the
  line, with the frames `run_engine:744 > staged_read:31 > h:17`.

## Also in this change

These are from FIX191's review:

- **N3.** `engine-escaped-log` no longer KILLs its writer by the pid the writer wrote. The writer ends on state:
  its write fails once the master closes, or the stop file appears. By the time of the KILL, that pid might no
  longer belong to the writer.
- **N4.** The case's bound is 10 s, not 6 s. That gives margin on a loaded runner and stays under the 15 s an
  unbounded read takes.
