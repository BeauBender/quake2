# FIX201: check-image-decoder bounds its runs with the process supervisor

## What changed

- `tests/check-image-decoder.sh` runs every bounded program (the host tests, the leaks(1) probe and
  the `leaks --atExit` run) through `scripts/lib/supervise.sh` (TASK218) in the foreground, with
  `--timeout N --grace 2 --events 9`.
- Deleted from the check: `reap`, the watchdog, the `kill -STOP` scan over `ps -A`, the `stopped`
  trap and the `IMGDEC_SELFTEST_DOGFILE` hook. The check holds no process-control code of its own.
- Status 124 is the bound ("outlived its N s bound"); 126 (a group the supervisor cannot empty after
  its KILL, or cannot go on watching) fails the check; an `escaped pid=Q` event fails the check, and
  every escaped pid is named. The leaks(1) probe's timeout
  still prints the Developer Mode line, and the skip and its `CHECK_IMAGE_DECODER_REQUIRE` guard are
  unchanged.
- The supervisor ends the group with TERM, a grace and KILL, so the target leaks(1) leaves stopped
  goes with it. A TERM or INT sent to the check alone does not reach the supervisor (the check has no
  trap); the supervisor's own deadline still ends the run.

## A TERM or INT to the check's own pid: accepted, not fixed

A TERM or INT sent to the check's pid alone, not its group, ends the check at once, and its EXIT
trap removes its scratch directory. The supervisor and the run in progress (with any target
leaks(1) holds stopped) go on until that run's deadline, `IMGDEC_LEAKS_PROBE_SECONDS` for the probe
and `IMGDEC_CHECK_SECONDS` (300 s by default) for the others. On main the check's `stopped` trap
ended them at once. This is accepted, for these reasons:

- The leftover is bounded: the supervisor's deadline ends the run's whole group, and nothing
  outlives it.
- The gate stops a check by its process group (`scripts/gate-checks.sh`), and a terminal's Ctrl-C
  reaches the whole foreground group. The supervisor is in that group and forwards INT, TERM and
  HUP to its run (TASK218), so these stops end the run at once.
- The card wants no process-control code in the check. A trap that forwards the signal to the
  supervisor would be such code.

Self-test case (l) pins it: a TERM to the check's pid ends the check with 143, the run ends at its
4 s deadline (the stub sees the supervisor's group stop no sooner than 2 s after it started), and
the fifo every member of the run holds reaches end of file, so nothing outlives the deadline. If a
change makes the run end at the check's TERM instead, case (l) fails and this section is updated
with it.
- `IMGDEC_SELFTEST_EVENTS` names a file the check appends the supervisor's events to. The self-test
  reads it.

## Self-test

Cases (a) to (e) stay. Cases (f), (g) and (h) of the old self-test (the reap function, a failing
ps(1), a TERM) and their decoys are gone. Two cases replace them, judged on events, with no sleep
and no ps poll: (f) a hanging stub, on a fifo nobody writes, is bounded and the events show
`deadline`, `group-empty`, `reaped` and no `escaped`; (g) a stub whose two helpers call setsid makes
the check fail naming every escaped pid.

Mutants (h), (i) and (j) each take one guard out of the check. (h) and (i) have positive controls,
so a mutant that never reaches the run it probes fails its case: (h) needs the stopped target's pid
in `stop.pids`, (i) needs the escape stub's helper in `esc.pids`.

Mutant (h) leaves two targets stopped (the probe's and the leak run's), with no supervisor to end
them. A process group that holds a stopped member gets SIGHUP and SIGCONT from the kernel when it
becomes orphaned (POSIX). In the self-test's own group that HUP ended the self-test and its run in
CI (status 129, "Hangup: 1" on the `run` line); orphaning that group while (h) holds a target
reproduces it locally. So (h) runs through `run_apart`: the check is in a process group of its own,
led by an anchor whose parent is the self-test, and the anchor outlives the check until
`end_apart` ends the whole group with one KILL. That group is never orphaned while a target in it
is stopped (its CONT would let the target run on and empty `stop.pids` of live pids), and the
self-test's own group never holds a stopped process, which (h) checks by process group.

On the CI runner (PR #788, `invariants_macos (fast)`) that fixed the status 129, but (h) then found
the probe's stopped target gone, so the mutant looked refused for the wrong reason. The real check
passed in the same run. Our reading, not proven there: on the runner the anchor's group is still
orphaned while it holds the stopped target, and the kernel's HUP and CONT end the target. So the
anchor records any SIGHUP it gets in `apart.hup` and exits. Nothing in the self-test sends HUP, so
only an orphaning or an outside sender can reach it. The inconclusive rule:

- The target gone and `apart.hup` present: (h) prints `(h) inconclusive here: the mutant's process
  group was orphaned and the kernel's SIGHUP ended its target; the case is proved where groups are
  not orphaned`, and does not count it as a failure.
- The same with `CHECK_IMAGE_DECODER_REQUIRE=1`: (h) fails.
- Every other verdict of (h) is unchanged: the positive control (no `stop.pids`), a stopped target
  in the self-test's own group, a target that outlives the end of its group, and the target gone
  with no `apart.hup`.

What is proved where:

- Where groups are not orphaned (a local run, plainly or in a new session with no terminal): (h)
  proves the plain-kill mutant is refused. With no orphaning, a mutant of `exit 3` in place of the
  supervisor line fails the positive control ("the mutant never started its target", status 3),
  and a mutant that keeps the supervisor fails with "the probe's stopped target is gone".
- Where the group is orphaned: (h) proves only that the orphaning was seen. Locally, sending the
  anchor's group SIGHUP then SIGCONT, as the kernel does, once its target is stopped, gives the
  inconclusive line and the self-test passes; with `CHECK_IMAGE_DECODER_REQUIRE=1` it fails.
- In CI, if (h) still fails with the target gone and no inconclusive line, no HUP reached the
  anchor: the reading above is wrong for that run, and the cause is elsewhere.

The self-test's EXIT cleanup ends every pid in `esc.pids` too, since an escaped helper is in a
session of its own and blocks on a fifo the cleanup removes. Case (k) runs the self-test with only
case (g) and a TERM to itself after that run, and waits for end of file on a fifo the helpers hold,
so it shows no helper outlives the interrupted self-test. Case (l) pins the accepted TERM behaviour
above.
