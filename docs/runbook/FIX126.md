# FIX126: t_settle reads what the settle did, not how long the put took

On the build VM on 2026-11-17 and 2026-11-18, gate runs on branches that do not touch the bench
failed `tests/check-bench.sh` on an upper bound on the put's wall time, at a load average of
about 150:

```
check-bench: settle: an unknown reading while settling waited 5 s
check-bench: failed: t_settle
```

The settle code was correct. The test was not. The card's other case, `t_lock_queue_reentry`,
was fixed by FIX128 ([its runbook](FIX128.md)).

## The cause

`t_settle` (`tests/check-bench.sh`) runs `bench.sh baseline put` against a stubbed GPU reader and
judged three of its sub-cases by how long the whole put took: an unknown reading had to end the
settle `within 4 s` (`_took -le 4`, twice) and a poll of 20 s had to be cut to a `gate_settle`
of 4 s `within 10 s` (`_took -le 10`). The put's wall time includes the stubbed snapshots, the
gate's reading and every process the put starts, so a loaded host lengthens it whatever the
settle does. The bounds measured the host's speed, not the settle.

## The load harness (removed)

The red and the green below were measured with a load harness, `tests/bench-load.sh`, which ran
a command N times beside `yes > /dev/null` busy loops (`-k` per core), optionally at a niceness
(`-p`). It was removed in this card's branch (commit e02ea3fe) at the owner's call: this host's
load is controlled by the heavy.sh lock, so stress runs are not wanted. The measurements stay
here as history. The version the nice 20 runs used can still be read with
`git show 608349a2:tests/bench-load.sh` (the nice 0 runs used c81870b6's, which had no `-p`).

Two limits of those runs, found in review: the measured command ran as a background job of a
non-interactive shell, so it started with INT ignored, and `check-bench.sh` skipped
`t_lock_stop`'s Ctrl-C cases in the "all cases" rows (its `INT_OK` probe); and a busy loop forked
just before a signal could be left unrecorded and outlive the harness.

## The stub proof

The red also shows without load. A copy of each `check-bench.sh` whose snapshot reader first
naps `SLOW_READ` seconds (one line added after `m=$1` in the `snapstub` it writes; unset, the
copy is the original) stands for a host that takes seconds between two readings. With
`SLOW_READ=3`, idle, at branch commit 608349a2 (main's copy from `origin/main` 8e7ce3f6):

- main's `t_settle`: failed (exit 1). Besides the three wall-time bounds (`an unknown reading
  waited 10 s`, `an unknown reading while settling waited 13 s`, `a poll of 20 s was not cut to
  the settle time of 4 s (status 1, 18 s)`), the falling reading was refused: its
  `gate_settle=5` ran out before the third slow reading, which is the same wall-time dependence
  in the case's input;
- the fixed `t_settle`: passed (`check-bench: ok (t_settle; no mutants)`);
- main's copy with `SLOW_READ` unset: passed, so the added line changes nothing on its own.

## The fix

`t_settle` no longer bounds the time a put takes. It reads what the settle did:

- the put runs with a `sleep` on PATH (`$E/napbin/sleep`) that records each nap's length in
  `$E/naps` and then runs the real `sleep`;
- an unknown reading, at once or while settling, must end the settle: the GPU reader was called
  once (the gate's reading alone) or twice (the gate's and the unknown one), from `n.gate`;
- a poll of 20 s with `gate_settle=4` must be cut: no nap in `$E/naps` is longer than 4 s, and
  at least one nap of 1 to 4 s is there, so a settle that took no nap fails too;
- the cases that must end early now have `gate_settle=60`, so a settle that did not end at once
  would read the GPU again and fail the count, long before the put's own bound (`wait_gone 40`)
  reaps it.

The one wall-clock check left is a lower bound (the put waited out `gate_settle=2`), which load
only lengthens.

## The mutants

The six `t_settle` mutants in `check-bench.sh`, from the idle full runs (the first five at
608349a2, all six at 4938c0c8 and 8a18270a):

| mutant | change (in `scripts/bench/core/`) | caught by `t_settle` on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `settle-no-poll` | `snap.sh`: no reading while settling | a reading that fell from 30 to 5 was refused |
| `settle-no-bound` | `snap.sh`: the settle has no time bound | a reading that stayed at 30 was not refused (status 99: the put's own bound reaped it) |
| `settle-not-flagged` | `snap.sh`: an unsettled run is not flagged | a reading that stayed at 30 was not refused (status 0) |
| `settle-nap-uncut` | `snap.sh`: a nap is not cut to the time left | a poll of 20 s was not cut to 4 s (a nap of 20 in `$E/naps`) |
| `settle-zero-polls` | `measure.sh`: the settle is skipped | a reading that stayed at 30 was not refused (status 0) |
| `settle-no-nap` | `snap.sh`: the settle takes no nap | the poll of 20 s took no nap |

All were caught; `settle-nap-uncut` is now caught by the nap log, not by the put's time.

## The other cases with wall-time bounds, left unchanged

- `t_gate`: the bounded wait must take `-le 20` s (`[ "$_t" -le 20 ]`).
- `t_prewarm`: a thermal warning must stop the prewarm `-lt 20` s after it started.
- The lock cases' `while [ "$(date +%s)" -le $((_tl + 3)) ]` loops (a wait of 3 s past a lock
  time).
- `t_gate_poll`: the gate with `gate_wait=4` must take under 4 + 6 s.

None of them failed in any run at niceness 0, up to a load average of 187, and none failed on the
build VM, so this card leaves them unchanged. At nice 20 with 24 busy loops, two of them did fail,
once each, in the one run of every case:

```
check-bench: gate: the bounded wait took 23 s, not about 2
check-bench: gate poll: the gate with gate_wait=4 took 20.610 s, over its wait and one reading
check-bench: failed: t_gate t_gate_poll
```

They are upper bounds on the wall clock like `t_settle`'s were, and nice 20 starves the self-test
harder than any load the gate has met. They are left for a follow-up card, which would give them
the same treatment (count the readings and naps the gate took). `t_prewarm` and the lock loops
passed in that run too.
