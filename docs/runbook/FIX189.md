# FIX189: the ios-sim selftest's decoy is in a group of its own from the fork, held by a flag, and says what ended it

## What happened

Twice, both times while other selftests ran at once, `scripts/ios-sim.sh selftest` failed in
`launcher-term-decoy` (its `st_decoy` check) with:

```
the decoy (pid N), which is not the hung call, was killed
```

Standalone runs passed. The case (FIX171) has the fake `xcrun` start a decoy, write its pid into
`hang.pid` with a `hang.cmd` that names another command, and hang; `st_signal` then sends the smoke a
TERM, and `st_decoy` checks that the decoy, which the stop path must not take for the hung call, still
runs.

## The trace

The decoy was started by the fake's `decoy 40`:

```
python3 -I -c 'import os, sys; os.setpgid(0, 0); os.execvp(...)' sleep "40.$$" &
echo "sleep 40.$$" > decoy.cmd; echo $! > decoy.pid
```

and then, in `hang1decoy`, `hang.cmd` and `hang.pid` (the decoy's pid) were written and the fake exec'd
its own `sleep 180.$$`. The old `st_decoy` printed "was killed" for every decoy that `ps` did not show
running `decoy.cmd`: one that was signalled, one that ended on its own, and one whose pid now ran
something else.

**(b) A real signal: the group stop reached the decoy before it left the group. This is the cause.**

- The fake `xcrun` runs as a `bounded` call (`bench_bounded`), the leader of a process group of its own.
  A child it starts is in that group. The decoy left it only when python ran its `os.setpgid(0, 0)`,
  after python had started: about 21 ms on an idle host (five runs of that python line, 21 to 23 ms),
  far more on a loaded one.
- The fake wrote `decoy.pid`, `hang.cmd` and `hang.pid` right after the fork, without waiting for that
  move. `st_signal` polls for `hang.pid` every 0.1 s and sends TERM as soon as it is there; the smoke's
  trap ends it, and `cleanup` stops the bounded call with `bench_stop_group`, a TERM to the whole group
  (`kill -s TERM -- -<pgid>`, FIX122). So between the fork and python's `setpgid` the decoy was a member
  of the group being stopped, and it got the TERM. That stop is correct: the primitive stops every
  member of the group it started, by design, so that a child `simctl` leaves behind is stopped too. The
  fault was the fake's claim that the decoy was in a group of its own when it named it.
- The FIX171 identity check (the pid in `hang.pid` is signalled only while it runs `hang.cmd`) plays no
  part: it guards the selftest's own KILL of the hung call, and the decoy never matched it. A reused pid
  is no cause of its own: the decoy's pid could be reused only after the decoy had ended.
- Reproduction: a scratch copy of `scripts/ios-sim.sh` whose selftest runs only the FIX171 decoy cases,
  with python's line changed to `import os, sys, time; time.sleep(1); os.setpgid(0, 0); ...` (the
  interleaving a slow python start gives). `launcher-term-decoy decoy` fails with exactly the field
  message, "the decoy (pid 69632), which is not the hung call, was killed", every run. The unchanged copy
  passes. (`hung-call-decoy`, whose fake call ends normally, passed in that staging; it was not traced
  further, since the field failure was in the signal case.)

**(a) The fixed lifetime ran out. Possible in principle, not what happened.**

- The same scratch copy with `decoy 2` instead of `decoy 40` gives the same message: "the decoy (pid
  40645), which is not the hung call, was killed". So the old message could not tell (a) from (b).
- But the 40 s were far from spent. Timed in the scratch copy (the decoy's `decoy.pid` mtime against the
  clock when `st_decoy` reads it), `st_decoy` looked 4 s after the decoy started in
  `launcher-term-decoy` and 2 s after in `hung-call-decoy`. For the lifetime to run out, the smoke would
  have had to take 37 s to exit on TERM, while `st_signal`'s own wait gives it 200 polls of 0.1 s and
  fails the case with "did not exit within 20 s" past that; neither sighting reported that failure.

Both are fixed, (b) in the fake (the stop path is right) and (a) by taking away the fixed lifetime.

## The fix

- **The decoy is in a group of its own from the fork on.** The fake starts it with job control on
  (`set -m`, then `set +m`): bash then puts the child in a group of its own in the parent too, before
  `$!` is known, so there is no window in which a group stop of the bounded call can reach it. The fake
  reads its group then, into `decoy.pgid`, and `st_decoy` fails a decoy whose group is not its own pid:
  "was not in a process group of its own when the fake named it".
- **The decoy is held by a flag, not a sleep.** It is `fakedecoy` (written by the selftest next to the
  fake `xcrun`, run as `/bin/bash <bin>/fakedecoy decoy.<pid>`, a command line unique to the fake's
  pid, which `decoy.cmd` holds). It lives until `st_decoy` creates `decoy.release`, or until its state
  directory goes (the selftest's EXIT trap); a cap of 6000 polls is there only for a selftest killed
  before its release.
- **The decoy records what ended it.** Its traps write TERM, HUP or INT to `decoy.sig`; its own end
  goes to `decoy.end` (`released`, or why it ended early). The fake names it (`decoy.cmd`,
  `decoy.pid`, then `hang.pid`) only once its traps are set (`decoy.ready`).
- **`st_decoy` gives three outcomes**: "was signalled (<SIG>)", "ended on its own before the release
  (<its record>)" or, with no record at all, "ended before the release and recorded neither a signal
  nor an end of its own (a SIGKILL, or a crash)"; and the pass, a decoy that ran its own command until
  the release and then ended `released`. It never says "killed". A second argument makes it a mutant
  check: the case passes only when the check fails with that reason.

## Cases and mutants

Two mutant cases, each `hangdecoy` with a fake variable, follow the FIX171 decoy cases:

- `hung-call-decoy-term` (`FAKE_DECOY=term`: the fake sends its decoy a TERM once it is named) must
  fail with "which is not the hung call, was signalled (TERM)";
- `hung-call-decoy-early` (`FAKE_DECOY=early`: the decoy ends by itself once it is named) must fail with
  "ended on its own before the release (the early mutant ended it)".

Red before the fix: with the new fake and the old `st_decoy`, both read "the decoy (pid N), which is
not the hung call, was killed", and the selftest of the decoy cases gave "2 of 8 cases failed". Green
after: "ok (8 cases)" for the decoy cases.

The interleaving, staged against the fix in the same scratch copy (`fakedecoy` starts 1 s late, before
its traps, and the fake no longer waits for `decoy.ready`):

- with the job control, `hung-call-decoy decoy` and `launcher-term-decoy decoy` pass: the late decoy is
  out of the stopped group. Only the term mutant fails ("recorded neither a signal nor an end of its
  own"), since its TERM now comes before the traps: that is what the `decoy.ready` wait is for;
- without it (`set -m` removed as well), both fail, each also with "was not in a process group of its
  own when the fake named it (its group: N)", the field cause with its true reason.

`tests/check-bounded-run.sh` pins one more marker reason for `scripts/ios-sim.sh`, "selftest: the term
mutant signals the decoy the fake started", for the mutant's TERM in the fake.
