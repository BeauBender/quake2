# FIX225: the fake reports a signal sent as soon as its hello is read

## What failed

A merge gate on a busy host failed `tests/check-supervise.sh` once, in 7 s, with
`FAIL int-ignored (/bin/bash): the leader said nothing (it exited), want sig=TERM`. Every other case
passed in that run. The change under gate was docs only.

## The race, traced

Line numbers are main's, `ccf5ef7c`, before this card.

The case is `c_int_ignored` (`tests/supervise/supervise_test.c:892`). The leader is the fake command
`tests/supervise/fake.c`, run by `scripts/lib/supervise.c`, which runs as a background job of a
`/bin/bash` that ignores INT (`supervise_test.c:119`, `(trap '' INT; exec "$@") & wait "$!"`). The
supervisor finds INT ignored at entry (`supervise.c:1000-1002`) and spawns the command with INT, HUP and TERM at their defaults
(`posix_spawnattr_setsigdefault`, `supervise.c:1070`).

1. The fake sends its hello, with the far end of its private channel, over the hello socket
   (`fake.c:134`, `sendmsg`).
2. The runner reads the hello (`supervise_test.c:336`, in `hello()`) and the `spawned` event (`leader()`,
   `supervise_test.c:603`).
3. The runner sends INT to the supervisor, `l->ppid` (`supervise_test.c:905`). The supervisor emits
   `ignored name=INT` and does not forward it (`supervise.c:798-801`); the runner reads that event
   (`supervise_test.c:906`).
4. The runner writes `D` on the test clock (`supervise_test.c:912`). The supervisor emits `deadline`
   (`supervise.c:879`) and stops the group: `kill(-leader, SIGTERM)` (`sv_group`, `supervise.c:481`), then
   `term-sent name=TERM` (`sv_stop`, `supervise.c:586`). The runner reads `term-sent`
   (`supervise_test.c:918`).
5. The runner reads a line from the leader's channel and wants `sig=TERM` (`supervise_test.c:922`).

Steps 2 to 4 wait only on the runner and the supervisor. Nothing in them waits on the fake past step 1.
The fake sets its channel (`fake.c:142`, `chan = sp[0]`) and its handlers (`fake.c:146-148`) only after
the `sendmsg`. On a host loaded enough that the fake is not run again between its `sendmsg` and its
`sigaction(SIGTERM, ...)` until step 4 is done, the TERM finds TERM at its default and ends the fake
with nothing written. The runner's `line()` then reads end of file on the channel
(`supervise_test.c:404`), and `expect_line` says "nothing (it exited)" (`supervise_test.c:658`).

The same window is in every case that signals a fake it has not yet sent a command to:
`signal-int`, `signal-term` and `signal-hup` (the leader), and `deadline`, `leader-first` and `cap` (a
forked fake). A forked fake inherits its parent's handlers, but closes its parent's channel and sets
`chan = -1` (`fake.c:211-212`) until its own hello is sent, so a signal in its window ends it at
`put`'s `_exit(70)` (`fake.c:61`), again with nothing said.

So the case was right to wait on the hello; the hello did not mean what the case took it to mean. The
supervisor is not wrong, and nothing in `supervise.c` changed.

## Reproduced with a held step

`fake.c` now sends the hello in `tell()` and sets the channel and the handlers in `arm()`. A build with
`-DFAKE_HOLD` holds at the end of `tell()`, with a `recv(..., MSG_PEEK)` on its channel, until the
runner writes to the channel or closes it. A signal sent in the hold finds the fake exactly as a
descheduled fake was found. `tests/check-supervise.sh` builds that fake and runs every case against it.

Red (commit `e075366e`, `tell` then `arm`, the old order), `tests/check-supervise.sh` with
`BENCH_LOCK=0`:

```
FAIL deadline (/bin/bash): the grandchild said nothing (it exited), want sig=TERM
FAIL signal-int (/bin/bash): the leader said nothing (it exited), want sig=INT
FAIL signal-term (/bin/bash): the leader said nothing (it exited), want sig=TERM
FAIL signal-hup (/bin/bash): the leader said nothing (it exited), want sig=HUP
FAIL int-ignored (/bin/bash): the leader said nothing (it exited), want sig=TERM
FAIL leader-first (/bin/bash): the member said nothing, want sig=TERM
FAIL cap (/bin/bash): the member said nothing (it exited), want sig=TERM
check-supervise: FAIL: a case failed against the held fake
```

The `int-ignored` line is the one the merge gate printed.

## The fix

`hello()` calls `arm(sp[0])` before `tell(&mh, sp)`: the channel and the handlers are set before the
hello is sent, so a fake whose hello the runner has read reports any INT, HUP or TERM it gets. The
`dfl=` and `mask=` fields of the hello are still read before `arm`, so they still report the state at
entry. A signal that comes after `arm` and before the hello is written to the channel's buffer, and
the runner never sees that fake, which is what happened before too.

No case changed: each still waits on the supervisor's events and the fakes' channels, with no sleep
and no poll. The fix is in the fake, not in the supervisor's signal handling, so no disposition probe
under `/bin/bash` 3.2.57 was needed.

## The mutant

`late-arm` moves the one line `arm(sp[0]);` back after `tell(&mh, sp);` in a copy of `fake.c` (sed
holds the line and appends it after the other; the check requires exactly one line removed and one
added, and each anchor occurs once in `fake.c`), builds it as the held fake, and runs `int-ignored`
alone:

```
check-supervise: fake mutant late-arm fails int-ignored: the leader said nothing (it exited), want sig=TERM
```

The seven `supervise.c` mutants and the two helper mutants are unchanged, and each still changes
exactly one line (the check refuses any that does not). The check's header now says seven, not six.
